# Writing quality

Use this guide when drafting a post, customer email, proposal, report, or similar piece of writing. Start with the audience, the purpose, and the relevant facts. Follow the user's explicit instructions and any applicable channel or brand guidance.

Before returning or saving the draft, review it once:

- **Purpose.** Check that each section does a job for the reader. Put the main point, decision, or request where they can find it.
- **Specificity.** Find passages that could fit almost any situation. Replace them with details from the brief or sources that help this reader understand or act. Leave gaps visible when the evidence is missing.
- **Selection.** Cut repeated points and background the reader already has. Keep the examples and qualifications that change how the reader interprets the message.
- **Fidelity.** Preserve the user's point of view and the strength of the evidence. Keep facts, commitments, citations, and uncertainty intact. When matching a personal or brand voice, use approved examples for the relevant channel when available.

Revise only where the review finds a meaningful problem. For example, after a pricing call, put the pricing decision and the agreed next step near the top of the follow-up. A recap belongs there only if it helps the recipient act.

The guide draws on Meta's finding that AI reviewers can reward polished, comprehensive prose even when an expert writer would leave details out. [Meta AI's Unslopping AI research](https://facebookresearch.github.io/RAM/blogs/unslop/)
